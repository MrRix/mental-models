# Models Index

## Decisions
- [[Base Rates and the Outside View]]: Start from how similar cases turned out, then adjust.
- [[Circle of Competence]]: Knowing where judgment stops matters more than how far it reaches.
- [[First Principles Thinking]]: Rebuild from irreducible facts instead of copying what others do.
- [[Inversion]]: Solve problems backward by asking what guarantees failure.
- [[Occam's Razor]]: Among equally good explanations, the one assuming least wins.
- [[Planning Fallacy]]: Forecasts for one's own plans run optimistic because plans describe the best case.
- [[Pre-Mortem]]: Imagine the plan already failed, then explain why.
- [[Regression to the Mean]]: Extreme results drift toward average once the luck is redrawn.
- [[Resulting]]: Grading a decision by its outcome instead of its odds.
- [[Sunk Cost Fallacy]]: Past spending, not future returns, keeps the project alive.
- [[Survivorship Bias]]: Learning only from winners because the losers vanished from view.
- [[The Map Is Not the Territory]]: Models, metrics and words simplify; they are never the thing itself.

## Systems
- [[Brooks's Law]]: Adding people to a late project usually makes it later.
- [[Chesterton's Fence]]: Learn why a rule exists before removing it.
- [[Goodhart's Law]]: Once a number becomes a target, it stops measuring the goal.
- [[Lindy Effect]]: For nonperishables, the longer something has lasted, the longer it lasts.
- [[Parkinson's Law]]: Work, and the staff doing it, expand to fill what's allowed.
- [[Second-Order Thinking]]: Ask "and then what?" to see the effects of effects.

## Psychology
- [[Anchoring]]: The first number considered drags later estimates toward it.
- [[Availability Heuristic]]: Judging frequency by how easily examples come to mind.
- [[Confirmation Bias]]: Seeking and remembering evidence that favours what is already believed.
- [[Hanlon's Razor]]: Error explains most harm that looks like malice.
- [[Hindsight Bias]]: Known outcomes feel predictable; memory rewrites what was foreseen.
- [[Incentive-Caused Bias]]: People sincerely come to believe whatever they are paid to believe.
- [[Representativeness Heuristic]]: Judging probability by resemblance, ignoring base rates and sample size.
- [[WYSIATI]]: The mind treats what it has as complete; confidence follows coherence.

## Economics
- [[Loss Aversion]]: Losses weigh more than equal gains against a reference point.
- [[Margin of Safety]]: A buffer so that being wrong isn't fatal.
- [[Opportunity Cost]]: The real price of a choice is the best alternative forgone.
- [[Reflexivity]]: Beliefs move prices, prices move fundamentals, and the loop overshoots.

## What it is
A model, word, number, or plan stands for something; it is not the thing itself. Every map leaves most of reality out, which is exactly what makes it usable. Trouble starts when the map is treated as complete, or still followed after the ground has moved.

## Origin
Alfred Korzybski, a Polish-American engineer and founder of general semantics, wrote "A map *is not* the territory" in a paper delivered in New Orleans in December 1931 (sources name either the AAAS or the American Mathematical Society, which met together). *Science and Sanity* (1933) states that a correct map shares "a similar structure to the territory." Mathematician E. T. Bell published "the map is not the thing mapped" in 1933. Lewis Carroll mocked a mile-to-the-mile map in 1893. The popular slogan drops Korzybski's point that shared structure is why maps work.

## Why it works
Any representation compresses reality, and minds reason on the compressed version because it is all they can hold. Errors live in two places: the gap between what was kept and what mattered, and the lag since the map was drawn.

## Best used when
- A metric, forecast, or spreadsheet is driving a decision.
- Conditions have shifted since the model was built.
- Labels are doing the arguing.

## Limitations
It says every map is incomplete, not which omissions matter. Pushed too far, it excuses distrusting all models, though nobody acts without one.

## Avoid when
The map has a long, tested record in a stable domain, like tide tables.

## Counter-models and tensions
- [[Occam's Razor]] favours simpler maps, which leave out more territory.
- Borges's 1946 tale of an empire-sized map shows the opposite failure: a complete map is useless.
- [[Reflexivity]]: in markets, the map people hold changes the territory.

## Who used it
- Gregory Bateson built on it in *Steps to an Ecology of Mind* (1972).
- Long-Term Capital Management collapsed in 1998 when models fitted to past correlations met a market that changed.

## Trigger question
When was this map last checked against the ground?

## Often confused with
- [[WYSIATI]]: a mental habit; map and territory covers any representation.
- [[Goodhart's Law]]: the narrower case of a measure turned target.

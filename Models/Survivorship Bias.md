## What it is
Drawing conclusions only from the cases that made it through a filter, because the ones that did not are out of sight. The sample looks like the whole population, but the very outcome being studied is what picked it.

## Origin
Cicero tells of Diagoras, shown votive paintings from sailors who prayed and survived storms, asking where the paintings of those who prayed and drowned were. The modern statistical treatment is Abraham Wald's 1943 memoranda for Columbia's Statistical Research Group, modelling how damage on returning aircraft reveals where lost ones were hit. The famous version, with a bullet-hole diagram and officers about to armour the wrong spots until Wald stepped in, has little documentary support, as an AMS column on the legend shows. The term was standard in fund research by the early 1990s.

## Why it works
Failures leave no trace: closed funds drop out of databases and dead startups give no interviews. Everyone left shares the trait of having survived, so anything common among them looks like a cause, even if the failures had it too.

## Best used when
Studying winners: fund track records, habits of the successful, old buildings that "were built better," any dataset assembled after the losers were removed.

## Limitations
When failures are unrecoverable, the bias can be named but not fixed. Survivors sometimes do share real causes; the bias says the evidence is weak, not that the conclusion is false.

## Avoid when
The data already includes failures, as in a complete cohort, or survival itself is what is being measured.

## Counter-models and tensions
- [[Base Rates and the Outside View]]: count the whole reference class, losers included.
- [[Lindy Effect]]: deliberately reads survival as evidence of robustness.

## Who used it
- Wald's memoranda were reprinted in 1980 by the Center for Naval Analyses, which noted the method was still relevant.
- Brown, Goetzmann, Ibbotson and Ross (1992) showed that studying only surviving mutual funds can create the appearance of persistent skill.

## Trigger question
Where are the ones who didn't make it?

## Often confused with
- [[Selection Bias]]: the broader family; survivorship is the case where the filter is success.
- [[Availability Heuristic]]: judging by what comes to mind, not by what the data excludes.

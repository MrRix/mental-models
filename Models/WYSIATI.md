## What it is
Daniel Kahneman's acronym for "what you see is all there is." The mind builds the most coherent story it can from whatever information is at hand and treats that story as complete. Confidence tracks how well the pieces fit, not how many are missing.

## Origin
Kahneman coined it in *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011), in the chapter "A Machine for Jumping to Conclusions." Support came from Lyle Brenner, Derek Koehler and Amos Tversky (1996): participants who read only one side of a legal dispute predicted the outcome more confidently than those who read both.

## Why it works
System 1, the fast intuitive mode, works only with ideas that have been activated. Information that is absent is never activated, so it carries no weight at all. Fewer pieces fit more easily into a tidy story, which is why less information can yield *more* confidence. Kahneman uses it to explain overconfidence, framing and base-rate neglect.

## Best used when
- Explaining confident judgments built from a résumé, a pitch deck, or one side of an argument.
- A vivid, coherent story is competing with a dull statistic.

## Limitations
- It is an umbrella description, not a separately measured mechanism, so it can explain almost any error after the fact.
- Kahneman noted jumping to conclusions is efficient when usually right and cheap to correct.
- The same book's priming chapter has not replicated well, so the book's authority alone proves nothing.

## Avoid when
- Data were sampled deliberately and the gaps are known.
- Information was withheld on purpose; that is an incentive problem, not a quirk of perception.

## Counter-models and tensions
- [[Base Rates and the Outside View]] imports what the vivid case omits.
- [[Pre-Mortem]] and [[Inversion]] surface absent possibilities.
- [[Occam's Razor]]: the simple story is sometimes right.

## Who used it
- Kahneman's example: told only that a would-be leader is intelligent and strong, most readers judge she will lead well.
- Adversarial courts hear both sides by design, an institutional answer to it.

## Trigger question
What am I not being shown?

## Often confused with
- [[Availability Heuristic]]: judging frequency by ease of recall. WYSIATI ignores what was never presented.
- [[Confirmation Bias]]: seeking agreeable evidence. WYSIATI needs no seeking; it works on whatever arrives.
- [[Survivorship Bias]], the special case where the missing data are failures.

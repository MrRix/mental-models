## What it is
A pre-mortem asks a team, before a plan launches, to imagine the plan has already failed badly and to write down why. The result is a list of risks surfaced while there is still time to act on them.

## Origin
Psychologist Gary Klein says he developed it around 1990 to cut problems on his company's projects; his best-known write-up is "Performing a Project Premortem", *Harvard Business Review*, September 2007. Daniel Kahneman later popularized it in *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011). Klein grounded it in a 1989 study of "prospective hindsight" by Mitchell, Russo and Pennington. His article says this raises the ability to correctly identify reasons by 30%; the study actually counted about 30% more reasons and did not judge their quality.

## Why it works
Two levers. Certainty: explaining a failure treated as fact comes easier than speculating about one that might happen. Permission: in a committed team, doubts sound disloyal, but a pre-mortem makes finding flaws the assigned task, loosening groupthink and deference to the boss.

## Best used when
- The plan is drafted but not yet launched.
- The team is confident, cohesive or hierarchical.

## Limitations
- The evidence is thin. The main controlled test (Veinott, Klein and Wiggins, 2010) had 178 students judge an H1N1 response plan; the pre-mortem cut confidence more than listing pros and cons. Lower confidence is not proof of better outcomes.
- Imagined failures skew toward the vivid rather than the likely.
- It can become a ritual whose list nobody acts on.

## Avoid when
- No concrete plan exists yet.
- The decision is small and easily reversed.

## Counter-models and tensions
- [[Planning Fallacy]]: the optimism it is built to puncture.
- [[Hindsight Bias]]: the machinery it borrows, put to useful work.
- [[Base Rates and the Outside View]]: the complement, asking how often similar projects fail.
- [[Availability Heuristic]]: its built-in weakness.

## Who used it
- Gary Klein's firm, Klein Associates, with its clients.
- Daniel Kahneman, who championed it as a cheap debiasing tool.

## Trigger question
It is a year from now and this failed; what happened?

## Often confused with
- Post-mortem: analysis after a real failure.
- [[Inversion]]: the general habit; a pre-mortem is one structured application.
- Devil's advocate: one assigned critic, whereas a pre-mortem makes everyone a critic.

## What it is
When several explanations fit the evidence equally well, prefer the one that assumes the least. It is a tiebreaker for hypotheses, not a claim that the world is simple.

## Origin
The name honours William of Ockham (c. 1287 to 1347), an English Franciscan logician, but the famous line "entities must not be multiplied beyond necessity" is not in his surviving works. His closest wording translates as "plurality should never be posited without necessity". The popular phrasing comes from the Irish Franciscan John Punch in 1639, who called it a common Scholastic axiom. Aristotle, Ptolemy, Grosseteste and Aquinas stated versions earlier, and the "razor" label came later, so no single origin year holds.

## Why it works
Every added assumption is another place to be wrong, and extra free parameters let a theory fit anything, including noise. A theory that can fit anything predicts nothing. Simpler explanations are easier to test, so they fail fast when wrong.

## Best used when
- Competing explanations cover the same facts.
- A story keeps needing patches to survive.
- Choosing which hypothesis to test first.

## Limitations
- It ranks hypotheses; it does not prove them. The *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* finds every justification for simplicity contested.
- "Simple" is ambiguous: fewer entities and shorter laws often pull in opposite directions.
- Nature is under no obligation to be tidy.

## Avoid when
- The simpler option explains less. The tie must be real.
- Several independent causes are plausible, as in patients with multiple conditions.

## Counter-models and tensions
- [[Hickam's Dictum]]: patients can have several diseases at once.
- Walter Chatton's anti-razor: if fewer things cannot explain it, add more.
- [[First Principles Thinking]]: may show the simple story skips a necessary step.

## Who used it
- Isaac Newton's first rule of reasoning in the *Principia*: admit no more causes than are true and sufficient.
- Diagnostic parsimony in medicine: seek one disease behind many symptoms.

## Trigger question
What is the least that has to be assumed to explain all of this?

## Often confused with
- [[Hanlon's Razor]]: a narrower rule about intent.
- "As simple as possible, but not simpler": widely credited to Einstein, but that wording is unverified.

## What it is
First principles thinking breaks a problem down to facts that cannot be reduced further, then rebuilds a solution from those facts rather than from precedent. Its opposite is reasoning by analogy: doing what others do, with small variations.

## Origin
The idea is Aristotle's, from the 4th century BC. In *Metaphysics* Book V he lists the senses of *archē* (beginning, principle), including the point from which a thing is first known. His *Posterior Analytics* argues that demonstrated knowledge must rest on primary premises that are not themselves demonstrated. The business usage comes from Elon Musk, who in a 2012 interview with Kevin Rose contrasted first principles with analogy.

## Why it works
Analogy imports the constraints of whatever is copied, including ones that are only habit. Decomposing to physical, economic or logical fundamentals separates real constraints from conventional ones. Musk's example: battery packs cost around $600 per kilowatt hour, while their raw materials came to roughly $80. Everything between those two numbers was open to question.

## Best used when
- Costs or rules in an industry rest on habit rather than physics.
- The situation is new enough that analogies mislead.

## Limitations
- It is slow. Musk himself has said analogy is necessary for most of daily life.
- What counts as fundamental is a judgment, and it can be wrong.
- It discards tacit knowledge embedded in conventions.

## Avoid when
- The domain's conventions are hard-won, as in safety codes or clinical practice, and the reasoner lacks expertise.
- The decision is routine and low-stakes.

## Counter-models and tensions
- Reasoning by analogy: the opposite, and the everyday default.
- [[Chesterton's Fence]]: find out why a convention exists before tearing it out.
- [[Lindy Effect]]: long-lived practices may carry hidden value.
- [[Circle of Competence]]: marks where rebuilding from scratch is safe.

## Who used it
- Aristotle, in logic and natural philosophy.
- René Descartes, rebuilding knowledge from what survived radical doubt in *Meditations* (1641).
- Physicists computing properties *ab initio*, from basic laws.
- Elon Musk, on battery costs at Tesla.

## Trigger question
Which of these constraints is physics, and which is just how it has always been done?

## Often confused with
- Contrarianism: first principles analysis often confirms the convention.
- [[Inversion]]: reverses the problem instead of decomposing it.
